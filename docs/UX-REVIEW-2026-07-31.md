# Legal Search Hub — UX & Feature Review (2026-07-31)

Broad UX, UI, and feature review of the live production site (legalsearchhub.com),
performed by walking the app in a real browser session: homepage, search,
category filters, firm detail pages (sparse and rich), sign-up form, cities
dropdown, and the authenticated owner upgrade flow through to live Stripe
checkout. Observation only — no changes were made as part of this review.

**Scope limits (honest gaps in coverage):**

- Mobile/tablet layouts could not be verified — the browser window resize
  tooling reported success but the viewport never actually changed, so all
  findings are desktop (~1512px).
- No Premium-tier firm exists in production, so a real Premium listing page
  is unobservable — only its placeholder marketing.
- Keyboard focus-state visibility could not be properly audited via
  automation; explicitly unverified rather than passed.
- Screenshot IDs (`ss_*`) refer to the review session's captures and are
  kept as internal reference markers.

---

## WHAT WORKS WELL

1. **Visual identity is coherent and credible.** Navy/blue palette,
   consistent buttons, professional skyline hero. The upgrade modal's
   serif-headline treatment is genuinely more polished than most
   small-directory checkouts.
2. **Search is fast, relevant, and communicates.** "3 results for 'divorce
   custody'" with a Clear search link, auto-scrolled to results via
   `#results`. Matching against bio text worked — all three results were
   genuinely custody-relevant firms.
3. **Real Google ratings on cards.** "4.8 (748 Google reviews)" style social
   proof on nearly every card is the single biggest credibility feature the
   site has.
4. **Expectation-setting copy is honest.** Cities dropdown grays out
   Tucson/Flagstaff with "Coming soon"; signup says "Every submission is
   reviewed before it goes live"; claim form says "our team reviews every
   claim." No overpromising.
5. **The rich firm pages read very well.** Knapp & Roberts' About section is
   specific and substantive — named verdicts, real history. Where this
   content exists, the site feels like a real editorial directory, not a
   scrape dump.
6. **The upgrade flow works end-to-end.** Modal opens, Monthly/Annual toggle
   correct ($29/$299), both reach correctly-priced live Stripe checkouts
   (verified in an authenticated owner session).

## FRICTION POINTS

1. **Two-tier data quality is very visible.** New listings' detail pages are
   nearly empty — Torgenson Law is a name, one chip, and whitespace; the
   entire page text is ~20 words. Next to Knapp & Roberts, it looks like a
   different product. ~100 of 119 listings look like this.
2. **Maps appear blank on production.** Every firm page's map panel is a
   white box with only an "Open in Maps" button — but maps rendered fine on
   localhost. `NEXT_PUBLIC_GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` is absent from the Vercel
   production env list, which is the likely cause and would be a one-line
   fix plus redeploy.
3. **Street View images are a lottery.** Some are respectable buildings;
   others are a dirt lot with a utility pole, parking lots full of cars, or
   an empty alley presented as the firm's photo. For a trust-focused legal
   site, a bad photo may be worse than none.
4. **"0 Divorce firms."** A hero-promoted category is empty, and its empty
   state says "browse all firms below" when nothing is below — the page just
   ends. Divorce is likely the highest-intent consumer search on the site.
5. **Category pills give no feedback.** Clicking a hero pill filters the URL
   but leaves you stranded at the top of an apparently unchanged page — text
   search scrolls to results, pill clicks don't. Also, search and category
   filter are mutually exclusive: searching replaces `?practiceArea=`
   entirely.
6. **No signed-in state anywhere.** After completing magic-link sign-in, the
   nav still shows "Login / Register" while owner-only controls render
   below. There's no account indicator, no sign-out, no way to tell you're
   logged in.
7. **"General Law Practice" leaked into the hero.** The catch-all category
   deliberately meant to stay unpromoted appears as a hero pill (the pills
   are DB-driven), wrapping alone onto a second row with inconsistent
   styling, and is selectable in signup.
8. **Red "CLAIM" badge on ~every card.** Alarm-red, B2B-meaning ("this
   listing is claimable") on a consumer-facing grid — a person looking for a
   lawyer doesn't know what it means, and red reads as a warning. Plus a
   stray lone "Claim" text link floats at the top-left of firm pages looking
   like debris.
9. **"Continue to listing details" goes to payment.** The modal CTA promises
   listing details; the click lands on a Stripe charge page. Label and
   destination disagree at the most sensitive moment in the funnel.
10. **Sign-in failure modes are rough.** The rate-limited state shows only
    "Could not send the sign-in link — please try again," which is exactly
    wrong advice for a rate limit (retrying makes it worse). Images also pop
    in late on result cards (blank gray for ~3s), and firm pages repeatedly
    froze the automated browser tab — worth a page-weight/performance look,
    though real-user impact is unconfirmed.
11. **"Featured Listings" is 100% self-promotion.** The homepage's most
    prominent section is three "Feature Your Firm Here" ads. Honest, but it
    means the flagship section markets the site instead of showing lawyers —
    and it will stay that way until the first Premium customer.

## FEATURE GAPS (observed, not generic)

1. **No sorting or secondary filters.** With 40+ personal-injury firms in
   one unordered grid, there's no sort-by-rating, no neighborhood/ZIP
   filter, no map view — the address data to support this already exists.
2. **Ratings are dead ends.** "4.8 (748 Google reviews)" isn't a link — you
   can't read a single review on-site or click through to Google. The trust
   signal stops one click short.
3. **No way to contact any firm through the site today.** The lead form is
   Premium-only and zero Premium firms exist, so effectively 100% of
   listings offer only a phone number and external website. Every consumer
   inquiry the site generates is currently untracked value leaking out.
4. **No save/compare/share affordances.** Choosing a lawyer is a comparison
   task across days; there's nothing to shortlist or revisit — not even a
   visited-state distinction on cards.
5. **No account surface at all post-login.** No dashboard, no "my listing"
   link, no sign-out — an owner who logs in must know to navigate back to
   their own firm's public URL to manage anything.
6. **Empty categories have no fallback inventory.** "0 Divorce firms" could
   at least show Family Law firms (the adjacent category with real
   inventory) rather than a dead end.

## UI ADDENDUM — Visual Design Assessment

### Typography

- Two competing type personalities: the site UI is one modern sans-serif,
  while the upgrade modal introduces an elegant serif for its headline and
  price. The serif treatment is the most premium-feeling typography on the
  site — but appears *only* there, hidden behind an owner-only button.
- Heading hierarchy on firm detail pages is thin: firm name (large bold)
  drops straight to small body text with little intermediate structure. On
  sparse pages there's effectively one type size doing all the work.
- The hero headline is well-sized with good drop-shadow contrast against
  the sky.

### Color

- The core palette is disciplined — navy, mid-blue, white, muted grays —
  consistently applied to buttons and links. Reads "law firm appropriate."
- Three accent colors compete without clear roles: red search button, red
  CLAIM badges, gold/navy PREMIUM badges. Red does double duty as both
  "primary action" (Search) and "status label" (CLAIM) — opposite jobs for
  the loudest color on screen.
- The upgrade modal introduces a fourth accent family (brown/tan `#8B7355`
  + cream) seen nowhere else — handsome in isolation but a different design
  system than the blue site around it.
- Hero pill contrast is a real accessibility issue: translucent pills with
  white text over light sky and building glass — "Immigration" and
  "Business Law" are borderline unreadable.

### Spacing & Layout

- Card grids are evenly guttered and aligned; the 3-column rhythm holds
  across Featured, All Firms, and results.
- Vertical dead space is the main problem: bare detail pages have a left
  column ~80% empty against a tall right sidebar — the two-column layout
  only earns its keep when a firm has a bio and gallery.
- Cards without bios collapse unevenly next to cards with bios in the same
  row — mismatched heights with visible blank slots.
- The lone "General Law Practice" pill orphaned on its own second row under
  eight pills is a visible layout imbalance on the most-seen screen.

### Component consistency

- Buttons: at least four styles in circulation — solid blue rounded, red
  pill (Search), outline (View All Firms / List Your Firm), and the modal's
  brown full-width. Blue solid dominates listings ("View Profile" ×119),
  flattening visual priority: every card screams equally.
- Badges: CLAIM (red, sharp corners) and PREMIUM (navy/gold) don't share a
  shape language.
- The firm detail page's top row mixes three interaction weights
  side-by-side — ghost text link ("Claim"), text link ("Claim This
  Listing"), solid button ("Go Premium →") — for essentially two actions.

### States (hover/focus/loading/empty)

- Loading: cards show flat gray boxes for ~3 seconds before images pop in —
  a shimmer/skeleton exists in code but what renders reads as "broken
  image" until it resolves.
- Focus states: not properly audited via automation — unverified rather
  than passed.
- Empty states: text-only, no illustration or visual treatment, functional
  but bare against an otherwise image-heavy site.
- The selected pill state (white fill) is clear once seen — but it's the
  *only* feedback filtering gives, which is why the no-scroll issue feels
  like a dead click.

### Overall UI verdict

The foundation is a clean, credible two-color system applied consistently
to layout and cards — above average for a directory MVP. The gaps are at
the edges: accent colors without assigned roles, the modal styled as its
own island, one real contrast failure (hero pills), and uneven card/page
density caused by data rather than design. None of it requires a redesign;
it needs a small set of rules (one accent for action, one for status; one
serif decision; minimum card content) applied everywhere.

---

## Highest-leverage fixes (reviewer's ranking)

1. **Production Maps key** — trivial env fix, repairs every firm page.
2. **Divorce empty category** — seed it or fall back to Family Law.
3. **Signed-in indicator + sign-out** — closes the most visible
   "unfinished" signal for owners.
